Critical: full outage, system/site/internet down, critical failure, severe business impact, SOS source. High: productivity blocked, multiple users, time-sensitive/immediate attention, significant business impact, user unable to sign in/work, termination/offboarding, Urgent in subject/title, VDI blackout. Moderate: access-point/TLA-AP or localized infrastructure issues. Low: routine single-user/minimal-impact requests and non-actionable release, content, DMARC, uplink, or network-speed reports. Informational: monitoring-only, disk, DFSR, Queue 210, or no-active-impact alerts. Select the highest justified level; consider impact before keywords and preserve a current priority unless a higher one is justified.

DATTO RMM NORMAL-PRIORITY OVERRIDE: If a ticket was created by DattoRMM API and its description explicitly marks the request’s urgency or priority as Normal—for example, “How urgent is your request?: Normal”—select Low or Moderate only, based on documented impact.
Exceptions: Do not apply this cap when the ticket documents a full or critical outage or a site-wide, company-wide, widespread, everyone, or all-users event. For those exceptions, use the standard priority matrix and assign High or Critical when warranted.

SECURITY-EMAIL PRIORITY OVERRIDE: Notwithstanding the standard matrix and the Datto RMM Normal-Priority Override, user-reported phishing, sextortion, or suspected account-compromise emails are High priority when they allege device or account access, spyware, credential theft, extortion, payment demand, or data exposure. Assign High because they require prompt human security review even when user interaction or compromise is not yet confirmed. Assign Critical only for confirmed critical or widespread business impact, active compromise, or an outage.
